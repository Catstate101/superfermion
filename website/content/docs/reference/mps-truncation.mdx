title: "MPS Truncation Diagnostics"
description: "What the four truncation numbers mean, why the returned state is not normalised, and how bond_dim compares across engines."
---

Every two-qubit step in an MPS run can discard weight: the bond cap projects the merged tensor
onto at most `bond_dim` kept indices. SuperFermion records four numbers about that process and
returns the surviving state unnormalised. Read together they tell you whether a bond is large
enough; read separately they are easy to misread.

## The four numbers

| method | definition | range | how to read it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `discarded_weight()` | Σε over every two-qubit step | ≥ 0, can exceed 1 | accumulated discard, **not** a fraction of the whole |
| `max_discarded_weight()` | largest single-step ε | 0 … 1 | the spike detector |
| `truncation_events()` | steps that discarded above the tolerance | integer | a count, not a severity |
| `fidelity_lower_bound()` | `clamp(1 − Σε, 0, 1)` | 0 … 1 | valid but loose; `0.0` means only that Σε ≥ 1 |

On a 16-qubit, 12-layer brickwork circuit at bond 16:

```python
import numpy as np
import superfermion as sf

ir = qc.to_ir()
m = ir.evolve_mps(16)

m.discarded_weight()        # 0.329799
m.max_discarded_weight()    # 0.090469
m.truncation_events()       # 15
m.fidelity_lower_bound()    # 0.670201
```

These values — and the bond ladder under [Choosing a bond](#choosing-a-bond),
which is the same circuit — come from the 0.1.18 kernel. Two later fixes, a rank
clamp in the block-diagonal merge and the mixed-canonical gauge before a
truncating merge, change *which* weight a given bond discards, so re-read these
four numbers on the build you are using (see
[The same cap in three engines](#the-same-cap-in-three-engines)). The definitions
and the reading rules below are unchanged.

## The returned state is a survivor, not a normalised state

`to_statevector()` and `mps_todense()` return the state the bond cap left behind. It is not
renormalised, so its squared norm is the survivor weight:

```python
psi = np.asarray(m.to_statevector())
norm2 = float(np.vdot(psi, psi).real)      # 0.828381
ir.mps_norm_sq(16)                          # 0.828381 — same number

# High-level handles answer the same question without densifying 2^n values.
# Works on every lane (statevector, density_matrix, stabilizer, mps).
st = sf.cat(qc, mode="mps", bond_dim=16).simulate({})
st.norm_squared()                           # the same number, without densifying

exact = np.asarray(ir.simulate_to_state("statevector").numpy())
fidelity = abs(np.vdot(exact, psi)) ** 2 / norm2   # 0.832776
```

`State.norm_squared()` is exact to `4.4e-16` relative against the densified sum on
`mps` handles and contracts the chain in place — it previously raised `MethodError`,
which forced callers to either densify (O(n·2^n·D²)) or divide by the wrong
normalisation. `purity()`, `probabilities()` and `fidelity(self)` renormalise and are
*not* substitutes when you want the survivor weight.

Dividing by `norm2` is not optional when comparing against an exact reference. Without it the
overlap is scaled by the survivor weight and the comparison is meaningless. For this circuit the
weight actually lost is `1 − 0.828381 = 0.171619`.

## Why Σε can exceed 1

Each ε is measured against that step's own tensor norm, not against the initial state, so the
per-step fractions do not add up to "the fraction of the state that was lost". The 16-qubit
brickwork at bond 2 measures `discarded_weight() = 4.8026` while the state still retains part of
its norm. For the total loss, read `1 − mps_norm_sq(bond)`; Σε is a step counter with weights.

## The bound is valid and loose

Because each step shrinks the norm by `(1 − ε_i)`, the survivor weight telescopes to
`‖ψ‖² = Π(1 − ε_i)`, which is at least `1 − Σε`. At bond 16 that is `0.670201 ≤ 0.828381`, and
the measured fidelity `0.832776` sits above both. This is why the method is called a *lower*
bound: it is conservative, empirical, and not a certified bound. When it prints `0.0`, the only
conclusion is Σε ≥ 1 — not that the state is orthogonal to the reference.

## The same cap in three engines

At a fixed cap the three engines now keep the same weight, because each
truncates the Schmidt tensor of its own cut. A 16-qubit, 12-layer random
brickwork (948 gates), one call per arm, bond 128:

| engine | setting | 1 − fidelity | ms |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| SuperFermion `cat` MPS | `bond_dim=128` | 9.88e-03 | 1,655 |
| Qiskit Aer | `matrix_product_state_max_bond_dimension=128` | 9.88e-03 | 4,176 |
| PennyLane `default.tensor` | MPS at bond 128 | 9.88e-03 | 19,315 |

`n = 20` repeats it: `4.18e-02` for SuperFermion and PennyLane, `4.22e-02` for
Aer, whose third digit differs because it renormalizes (`norm² = 1.003`) and
breaks ties differently. The times are one window on one host and are **not** a
speedup of record — the PennyLane arm has read 5.1x slower than a previous
session on unchanged code — so the accuracy column is the claim.

At caps below 128 the engines stop agreeing, and the direction is informative. Same circuit,
against the exact state, `n = 16`:

| bond | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SuperFermion, 1 − fidelity | 0.9528 | 0.7702 | 0.4354 | 0.1176 | 9.88e-03 |
| PennyLane, 1 − fidelity | 0.9528 | 0.7702 | 0.4354 | 0.1176 | 9.88e-03 |
| Qiskit Aer, 1 − fidelity | 0.9597 | 0.7820 | 0.4408 | 0.1180 | 9.88e-03 |

SuperFermion and PennyLane return the same state at every cap, survivor norm included
(`0.049479`, `0.232135`, `0.565862`, `0.882611`, `0.990128`). Aer is slightly *less* accurate
below 128 because it renormalises after each cut (its `norm²` reads `1.059` at bond 8, `1.027`
at 16, `1.011` at 32, `1.000` at 64 and 128). A low cap is inaccurate in every engine; below
128 the ordering is SF = PL, and Aer is the one that differs.

Two fixes put that column together, both on by default: the block-diagonal merge
clamps its kept rank by the rank of the merged tensor (it had inflated the bond
past it), and the chain is held in the mixed-canonical gauge before a merge that
can truncate (it had truncated a non-Schmidt tensor). `SF_MPS_GAUGE=0` restores
the pre-gauge behaviour for an A/B.

An earlier version of this section measured a gap at bond 16, before both fixes.
What still does not travel is the *number*: `bond_dim` is a cap on indices, and
the fidelity it buys depends on the Schmidt spectrum at the circuit's worst cut,
so a bond that is exact for one circuit truncates another. Compare each engine
against a dense reference at the bond you intend to use, not against another
engine's bond number.

## Where the truncating-cap time goes

A cap that truncates is slower than a rank-limited cap at the same width: at `n = 16`,
bond 128 takes 1,044 ms against 487 ms at bond 256 (interleaved, four threads). The
intuitive explanation — "it pays SVD selection even when nothing is dropped" — is only a
third right. Per-merge instrumentation (`SF_MPS_SVD_DEBUG=1`) prints one line per merge attempt; an exact
split that its own `ε > 1e-10` test rejects is retried on the SVD path, so lines are not
merges. That cell (build #29, before the pivoted split added inside the exact path),
n = 16, bond 128, 12-cycle brickwork:

| | count | note |
|---|---:|---|
| merge attempts (lines) | 150 | 124 merging gates plus 26 retries |
| exact block-diagonal split | 81 | 55 accepted outright, 26 rejected |
| thin SVD | 69 | 43 direct + 26 retries; the large merges, mean `min(nrows, ncols)` = 41.7 |
| ... of those, every direction kept | 64 | `k == r`, so nothing was dropped |
| merges that drop material weight | 5 | largest 7.6e-3, each fires the measured-loss guard |

Of the 1,035 ms of merge work: 397 ms factorisation, 173 ms factor rebuild, 116 ms guard
QR, 40 ms bookkeeping.

* **The factorisation is not skippable by asking "is the cap binding?"** — `k` is an
  *output* of the spectrum, and the spectrum is also what prunes phantom bonds. Routing
  `min(nrows, ncols) <= bond_dim` merges into the QR split instead was implemented and
  reverted: the unpivoted row cut perturbs near-degenerate Schmidt spectra (a dirty bond
  ladder {34, 42, 52, 64}, ~5e-4 error, and slower). `SF_MPS_TRUNCATION=qr` still exposes
  that path for an A/B; it is not the default for this reason. What *is* avoidable is the
  SVD on merges the split's own row selection cannot certify — see the last bullet.
* **The guard is accuracy-critical.** It re-selects by measured `‖M·v_c‖` when the reported
  σ and the factors disagree, which is what keeps `discarded_weight` honest on the 5 merges
  that actually cut.
* **The factor rebuild was pure overhead, and is fixed.** With `k == r` the old code
  rebuilt the factors with `r` scalar matvecs; it now does one BLAS-3 product. Interleaved
  A/B on the same cell: 1,108 → 1,044 ms at bond 128 (−5.8%) and 563 → 487 ms at bond 256
  (−13.5%), with `1 − fidelity` between the two builds at 0 to 2.2e-16.
* **The 26 rejected retries are gone (current build).** A row *subset* cannot certify a
  merge whose mass sits on more rows than its rank, even when that rank fits the cap, so
  the exact split now falls back to a column-pivoted QR of the same middle matrix: it keeps
  all `2·d_r2` columns, is exact by construction, and measured `ε = 0` on all 26 merges at
  n=16/bond 128. That cell now makes **43** thin-SVD calls per run, not 69. Interleaved at
  two pinned threads: 1,796 → 1,705 ms at bond 128 and 833 → 582 ms at bond 256, with
  `1 − fidelity` between builds at `-2.2e-16`; `SF_MPS_BD_PIVOT=0` reproduces the previous
  build bit for bit. Accuracy does **not** move — those merges were already exact under the
  SVD (`k == r`), so read this as time saved, not as a defect repaired. Report §46.21.10.

If you monitor `truncation_events`, read it together with `discarded_weight`: the counter
increments once per merge with ε > 1e-15, so merges sitting at machine noise flip in and
out. On one binary the same cell reports 12 events at four threads and 10 at the default
thread count, while ε agrees to 13 digits (`9.89002031524577e-03` vs `9.89002031524981e-03`).
The weight is the stable number; the count is a threshold tally and is not comparable across
thread settings. Full tables and the build hashes: report §46.21.9 and §46.21.10,
reproduced by `_sfpl_bench/_mps_selcost.py wall` / `attrib`, `_sfpl_bench/_ab_time.py` and
`_sfpl_bench/_bd_pivot_check.py`.

## Gradients through a truncated chain

`mode="mps"` handles differentiate too, with the exact shift rule on the chain
rather than a dense reverse sweep. At a bond large enough to be exact the answer
matches the adjoint lane to `2.3e-15` measured; once the cap discards weight the
shift rule differentiates the **survivor** functional — the state as returned,
without renormalisation — so a truncated gradient is the derivative of what the
cap kept, not of the ideal state. Raise the bond until `discarded_weight()` is
zero, or use `mode="adjoint"`, when the ideal state's gradient is what you need.
The cost is `2P` evolutions at an exact bond and `4P` once the cap bites; the
[gradients guide](/docs/guides/gradients#gradients-through-an-mps) has the
runnable example.

## Truncation policy

`SF_MPS_TRUNCATION` selects the algorithm:

- unset or `svd` — SVD truncation (default)
- `qr` or `legacy` — the historical QR position cut

It is read once per process and cached, so it must be set before `import superfermion`; changing
it in a running interpreter has no effect. The QR path is monotone in the bond but less accurate
at small caps (`0.652338` against `0.773392` at bond 14 on the circuit above). It exists as a
fallback for reproducing historical runs, not as a tuning knob.

## Choosing a bond

Raise the bond until fidelity stops moving, and check that it never decreases on the way:

| bond | 14 | 15 | 16 | 20 | 24 | 32 | 48 | 64 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| fidelity | 0.773392 | 0.788872 | 0.832776 | 0.899101 | 0.951217 | 0.989850 | 0.998546 | 1.000000 |

Monotonicity is the check that catches a bad cap without needing the exact state — a larger bond
must never return a worse state.

### Measured on 0.1.30: the ladder is not the old ladder

The table above is 0.1.18. The brickwork behind it — the seed-20261119 generator, which
reproduces the page's `0.832776` at bond 16 on the 0.1.18 wheel — gives a different, lower
ladder on the current kernel:

| bond | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 | 256 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| fidelity | 0.0472 | 0.2298 | 0.5646 | 0.8824 | 0.9901 | 1.0000 |

Bond 64 returns `1.000000` in the 0.1.18 table and `0.8824` here. That is the rank clamp and
the gauge fix acting on *this* circuit, not a regression being hidden: the older table is kept
for provenance, and you should re-measure a bond you carried over from 0.1.18.

At `n = 16` bond 256 is exact because a 16-qubit chain can have rank at most `2^8 = 256`; 256,
512 and 1024 all return the same state. That rank limit is why the cap stops mattering — and
why it cannot be a constant. At `n = 24` even bond 256 leaves a `1.1e-2` defect, and at `n = 32`
it still discards `0.72%`.

Depth moves the requirement while the qubit count stays fixed. `n = 16`, bond 128:

| cycles | gates | 1 − fidelity | bond for 1 − fidelity ≤ 1e-6 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 158 | 6.7e-16 | 128 |
| 8 | 632 | 6.3e-08 | 128 |
| 12 | 948 | 9.9e-03 | 256 |
| 16 | 1,264 | 6.0e-02 | 256 |
| 24 | 1,896 | 0.322 | 256 |
| 32 | 2,528 | 0.549 | 256 |

Bond 128 is exact to 8 cycles and retains only **44.7% of its norm** by 32 cycles. Choosing a
cap *at* the rank limit is also cheaper than one below it: at `n = 16`, bond 256 took 1,023 ms
against 2,020 ms for bond 128, because the truncating path pays for SVD selection the exact
path does not.

## Common mistakes

- **Passing an `IR` to `mps_todense`.** It takes a `Circuit`. For an IR, use
  `ir.evolve_mps(bond).to_statevector()`; the call now raises a `TypeError` that says so.
- **Comparing to an exact state without dividing by the survivor norm.** See above.
- **Reading `discarded_weight() > 1` as a bug.** It is a sum of per-step fractions.
- **Reading `truncation_events()` as a severity score.** A single catastrophic merge can leave
  *fewer* events than a benign one, because it removes most of the weight at once.

## Note on 0.1.17

The published 0.1.17 wheel can return a worse state at a larger bond: on this circuit bond 16
returned `0.003168` against `0.788872` at bond 15, with a single step discarding `0.9871`. The
cause was a row-prefix selection inside the exact block-diagonal split of the default SVD path;
it is fixed as of 0.1.18, which is what the numbers on this page come from, and 0.1.19 carries
the same fix. On 0.1.17, sweep a few bonds and check monotonicity before trusting any single cap.
